Part 1 / Analysis

Task 1: Order the alerts

The alerts are listed in the order they reached the SIEM, not the order they happened. Drag the cards, or use the arrows, to put them in chronological order by UTC, earliest first. The ticket number stays with the alert and says nothing about when it happened.

Task 2: Classify the alerts

The alerts are listed in the chronological order you set in Task 1, so you can read the sequence of events as you classify. For each alert, choose one category and write a short reason. Base the reason on the log and the baseline, not on the alert title.

  • Part of the attack: a step in the intrusion you are escalating.
  • Routine noise: matches the baseline or is normal background activity.
  • Unrelated incident: a security event worth its own review, but not part of this attack.

Part 2 / Escalation

Task 3: Escalation report

Write the report you would hand to Tier 2. Write it for a reader who has not seen the alerts: state what happened, what you are sure of, and what is still open. The guidance under each heading stays on this page; the exported PDF contains only your report.

1. Executive summary

What happened, in plain language a non-technical manager can follow: how the attacker got in, what they reached, and the business impact.

2. Scope

Hosts, accounts and data involved. Include what the attacker tried and failed to reach, and the alerts you excluded and why. Start a line with "- " to make a list.

3. Timeline (UTC)

One event per line, starting with the UTC time, for example "08:12 Phishing email delivered to y.cohen".

4. Findings

For each attack alert: the evidence and indicators in the log, and how it deviates from the baseline.

AlertEvidence and indicatorsDeviation from baseline

5. MITRE ATT&CK mapping

Tactic, technique ID and name, and why the alert fits. Map only what the log supports.

AlertTacticTechniqueJustification

6. Indicators of compromise

Domains, addresses, file names, paths, registry values and command lines another team can search for. Defang domains and URLs, for example example[.]com.

7. Actions taken

What you verified and what you did or requested before escalating.

8. Not checked

Work that still needs to be done, for Tier 2 to pick up.

9. Open questions

What the available logs cannot tell you. Mark conclusions that are inferences rather than facts in the log.

10. Recommendations

Immediate containment first, then longer-term fixes that would have stopped or detected this attack earlier.

11. Severity rationale

Why the severity you chose above is right.
